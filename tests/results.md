## User-test results

8 people took part between October 17 and October 23, 2026: Minerva grad (2), Bootcamp grad (2), Short-course grad (1), Job seeker (2), L&D professional (1). All 8 agreed to the opening notice. Each call ran about 25 minutes: problem check, upload and price screen, the prototype test, then needs and jobs.

### Task success

7 of 8 testers finished setup, in a median of 94 seconds (range 67 to 138). 6 of 8 finished the session, in a median of 305 seconds (range 285 to 338), so the session holds to its 5-minute promise. Across the 7 testers who reached the recall questions, 16 of 21 answers were right. Across the 6 who finished the scenario, 8 of 12 decisions were right.

- P6 (Minerva grad) stopped during the session: "I don't really see why I need to review this when I can just look it up when I need it."
- P8 (Job seeker) stopped during setup: "I didn't know which job title to choose because I'm applying to several different types of marketing roles."

### Where people got stuck, in their words

- P1: "I wasn't sure why it thought I forgot Python more than statistics."
- P2: "I didn't know if I was supposed to answer based on what I learned or what I would do at work."
- P3: "The skill names were a little too technical. I didn't recognize one even though I knew the topic."
- P4: "I don't understand how this score relates to whether I'm ready for a job."
- P5: "I'm not sure how the system distinguishes forgetting from never learning the skill properly."
- P6: "I don't really see why I need to review this when I can just look it up when I need it."
- P7: "Some design questions don't have one correct answer, so I wasn't sure why my choice was marked wrong."
- P8: "I didn't know which job title to choose because I'm applying to several different types of marketing roles."

### What the calls said about the problem

Answers about how often graduates need a skill they have not used in months:

- P1: Every few weeks. Usually looks through old notes or searches online because they remember the concept but not how to apply it.
- P2: Almost every week. They often forget syntax or how to work with certain frameworks and spend time reviewing tutorials.
- P3: About once a month. They usually watch parts of the course again because they have forgotten specific Excel or SQL functions.
- P4: Every few weeks during job applications. Sometimes they skip applying for jobs because they no longer feel confident about certain technical requirements.
- P5: Frequently, especially when employees attend training but do not use the material afterward. Managers often have to provide additional guidance.
- P6: A few times per year. Usually reviews old class materials when a project requires something unfamiliar.
- P7: Every month or two. They remember general design principles but struggle to explain why a particular design decision makes sense.
- P8: About twice a month. They sometimes realize they have forgotten marketing analytics terminology and need to review examples before interviews.

When a faded skill gets noticed: interview 4, project 3, new job or project 1. What matters more right now: learning new skills for the job 3, keeping skills fresh 2, applying a recent course 2, something else 1.

### The fade estimate, the schedule and the skill map

Fade estimate believable: medium 4, low 3, high 1. Reaction to the job-based skill map: positive 5, neutral 2, skeptical 1. Where 5 minutes fits: during breakfast or before starting work; before the daily job search; during a lunch break; between job applications; at the start of the workday or after training; during a commute, but unlikely to use it daily; during morning coffee; while commuting or waiting between activities.

### Saturation

New themes per call, in order: 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 2 (17 in total). Every call added at least two themes, so the stop rule (three calls in a row with none) is not met and interviews continue toward 15 to 20.

### Design changes from the calls

| Tester | Theme | Change for version 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Lack of academic structure after graduation, need for transparency in fade estimates. | Add an explanation of why each skill received its fade estimate. |
| P2 | Preference for practical tasks, interest in GitHub integration. | Include more workplace-based coding scenarios. |
| P3 | Unclear skill terminology, need for beginner-friendly explanations. | Add plain-language descriptions below technical skill names. |
| P4 | Privacy concerns about grades, difficulty understanding the relationship between skill scores and job readiness. | Allow users to upload course titles without sharing grades. |
| P5 | LMS integration, team-level reporting, difference between forgotten and never-learned skills. | Separate initial skill proficiency from estimated retention in the dashboard. |
| P6 | Preference for on-demand learning, resistance to daily study requirements. | Add an on-demand practice mode that does not require a daily session. |
| P7 | Subjective assessment, portfolio-based skill tracking. | Give feedback on the reasoning behind decisions instead of marking every scenario as simply right or wrong. |
| P8 | Multiple target roles, onboarding problems when job preferences are unclear. | Allow users to select multiple job roles during onboarding. |

These are findings about this prototype and these testers, used to change the product. They are not estimates for graduates in general.
